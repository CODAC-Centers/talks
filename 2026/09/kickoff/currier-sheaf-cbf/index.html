Control Barrier Functions (CBFs)

System dynamics
\[ \dot{x} = f(x) + g(x)u \]
Safe set construction
\[ \mathcal{S} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{n} : B(x) \leq 0\} \]
with the CBF candidate \(B(x) = [B_{1}(x), \ldots, B_{v}(x)]\), \(B: \mathbb{R}^{n} \to \mathbb{R}^{v}\).
Forward invariance under the closed-loop dynamics
\[ \dot{x} = f_{\mathrm{cl}}(x) = f(x)+g(x)\kappa(x) \]
\[ x(t_0)\in\mathcal{S} \implies x(t)\in \mathcal{S}\quad \forall t\geq t_0 \]

Vector-Valued CBFs

Set of admissible safe control inputs
\[ K_{\mathrm{CBF}}(x) \triangleq \{u \in \mathbb{R}^m : \nabla B(x)^{\top}[f(x) + g(x)u] \leq -\gamma(x)\} \]
QP step:
\[ \begin{aligned} \kappa^*(x) = {} & \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{u\in \mathbb{R}^{m}}\; \|u-u_{\mathrm{nom}}\|^2 \\ \text{s.t. } & \nabla B(x)^{\top}[f(x) + g(x)u] \leq -\gamma(x) \end{aligned} \]
\[ \kappa(x) \in K_{\mathrm{CBF}}(x) \]
Single-valued and continuous
\[ \begin{aligned} B_1(x,y) &= x - d \\ B_2(x,y) &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,x + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,y - d \\ B_3(x,y) &= y - d \\ B_4(x,y) &= -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,x + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,y - d \\ B_5(x,y) &= -x - d \\ B_6(x,y) &= -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,x - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,y - d \\ B_7(x,y) &= -y - d \\ B_8(x,y) &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,x - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,y - d \end{aligned} \]
Eight affine barrier functions \(B_1, \ldots, B_8\); their zero sublevel sets meet in an octagonal safe set.

Sheaf Compatibility Conditions

A local feedback law over an open set \(U \subseteq \mathcal{M}\) is a section of the control bundle. Given a rank-\(m\) vector bundle \((\mathcal{U},\pi,\mathcal{M})\), the map \(\kappa\colon \mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{U}\) is a section that admits a space of local sections \(\kappa \in \Gamma(U,\mathcal{U}) = \{\kappa\colon U \to \mathcal{U}|_U \mid \pi\circ\kappa = \mathrm{id}_U\}\).
\(\mathcal{U}|_U\) \(\kappa(x)\) \(\pi\) \(U\)
Presheaf:
\(\mathcal{F}(U)\triangleq\{\kappa\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{U})\mid\kappa\text{ is continuous}\}\)
\(\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{M})^{\mathrm{op}}\to \mathbf{Set}, \qquad U \mapsto \mathcal{F}(U)\)
To establish \(\mathcal{F}\) is a sheaf: for an open cover \(U=\bigcup_\alpha U_\alpha\),
Locality.
\(\kappa,\widetilde{\kappa}\in\mathcal F(U)\)
\[ \begin{gathered}\kappa|_{U_\alpha}=\widetilde{\kappa}|_{U_\alpha} \quad\forall\alpha \\ \Longrightarrow\quad\kappa=\widetilde{\kappa}\end{gathered} \]
Gluing.
\(\kappa_\alpha\in\mathcal F(U_\alpha)\)
\[ \begin{gathered}\kappa_\alpha|_{U_\alpha\cap U_\beta} =\kappa_\beta|_{U_\alpha\cap U_\beta} \quad\forall\alpha,\beta \\ \Longrightarrow\quad\exists!\,\kappa\in\mathcal F(U) \\ \text{such that }\kappa|_{U_\alpha}=\kappa_\alpha \quad\forall\alpha\end{gathered} \]
\(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{CBF}}(U) \triangleq \{\kappa \in \mathcal{F}(U) \mid \colorbox{#F4CCC8}{$\kappa(x) \in K_{\mathrm{CBF}}(x)$}\;\; \forall\, x \in U\}\)
➔
\(\kappa \in \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{CBF}}(U)\)

Conclusion

Core result
Compatible continuous local CBF controllers determine a unique global feedback law on an admissible domain.
Broader opportunity
A common language for local models and their compatibility, within control and beyond.
Compatibility matters on trivial and nontrivial bundles.
Current work
  • Develop computational synthesis and overlap consistency checks.
  • Connect continuous and discrete CBF constructions.
  • Evaluate computational performance and robotic implementations.
Next: take the local-to-global math framework to metriplectic dynamics and the HJB equation.

For me, the broader opportunity is the language this gives us for local models. The bundle tells us what kind of data lives over each state. Sections describe local choices of those data. The sheaf records restriction and compatibility. That organization could be useful beyond this particular control construction.

Compatibility matters even when the bundle is trivial. Local estimates or controllers can still disagree after they are expressed in a common frame. Dead reckoning and loop closure provide a useful analogy: when we revisit an area, overlapping local descriptions can reveal accumulated disagreement that we need to correct.

Exact gluing applies once the local data agree. A computational extension can ask how to measure and reduce disagreement when they do not. A disagreement is not automatically a topological obstruction, and correcting one does not by itself establish safety.

We are currently developing the computational extension and its connection to discrete CBF constructions. The goal is a common mathematical language that leads to useful synthesis tools, with computational performance and robotic implementation evaluated explicitly.

Technical qualifications. The glued law is unique relative to the specified compatible family, not unique among all safe laws. Continuity of pointwise optimizers is an analytical requirement, not an automatic consequence of convexity. Forward invariance needs the dynamical, viability, and continuation hypotheses. The computational and discrete connections are ongoing work, not a demonstrated equivalence or speedup. Valid bundle transition functions already satisfy their cocycle identities; it is local estimates/controllers, or noisy proposed transformations not yet forming a valid atlas, that can disagree.
